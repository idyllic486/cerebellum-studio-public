When one section is held out

Hold-out validation

Tests the five interior sections of the default seven. The contour and centroid of the held-out section are not used for prediction. Shape and centroid position are predicted from the two neighbouring sections and compared with the actual ROI.

Reference points provided by the atlas

  • Compare 1Cb–10Cb in near-midline Fig. 101 with Sim, Crus1, Crus2, PM and Cop in lateral Fig. 123 to examine changes in branching and folding.
  • Lobule and fissure names serve as references for comparing morphology. They have not been automatically assigned to the current ROIs.
  • The atlas provides sections in three orientations. This viewer cuts the model coordinates at arbitrary angles to examine orientation-dependent changes in shape.
  • The 40 μm section thickness and plate spacing are properties of the atlas specimen. The spacing and orientation of the example specimen must be verified separately.

Data and scope of interpretation

Uses the final 12 sections of the example specimen. The default range comprises the seven sections Nos. 1–37, retaining the exclusion of Nos. 73 and 79. Detached tissue from No. 43 onward retains its placement within the image.

The example specimen’s depth and axes are virtual coordinates. The separate atlas explorer uses published drawing coordinates and has not been registered to the example specimen. Source-data terms, atlas-based candidates and reviewer observation confirmations are distinguished. Area/PL path values are diagnostic candidate values and do not represent local layer thickness or cell counts.

Atlas outlines: Piluso et al., Imaging Neuroscience 2025 (CC BY 4.0), built on the Allen Mouse Brain Common Coordinate Framework v3 (Wang et al., Cell 2020).

Review records are stored temporarily in the browser; export JSON and keep it separately.

Registration is the process of aligning corresponding positions across datasets. A lobule is a small anatomical subdivision of the cerebellum.
